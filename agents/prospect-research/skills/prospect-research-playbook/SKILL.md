# Prospect research playbook

Produce a reason to call and a question that can disprove the reason. A useful brief helps the caller decide what to say; it does not imply the prospect has agreed with our diagnosis.

This combined operational skill uses five supplied books, prior book-to-skill analysis and verified public companions. Updated 2026-09-18 with the converter's fold-in workflow: Richter's 2008 book was extracted in full and relevant sections were synthesized. Coverage is selective; illustrations and every passage were not comprehensively checked. [sources.md](sources.md) records provenance and limits. These five workflow chapters are our synthesis, not the books' original chapter numbers.

## Use the right depth

Start with campaign objective, approved offering facts, target customer, contact identity, and previous conversation. Use the user's provided context; ask only when missing information materially changes the target or call purpose.

For an ordinary first touch, find one credible business signal and its connection to the target role. For a strategic account, invest in corroboration and stakeholder mapping. The default working budgets in [patterns.md](patterns.md) are operating choices, not author benchmarks. Stop when another search is unlikely to change the opener, eligibility, or first question.

If this host has search tools, research using the plan below. If it supplies only a research report, judge that evidence and state gaps; do not claim to browse or open pages. The sales engine currently supplies research to this agent rather than granting it browsing tools.

## Core method

1. **Choose the right account.** Apply Weinberg's finite, focused, written, workable target discipline. Compare the account with the actual campaign criteria; mark unknowns separately from mismatches.
2. **Resolve the right entity.** Match domain, location, business model, and professional role. A name match alone is insufficient. Do not combine similarly named companies or a person's old and current jobs.
3. **Apply Richter's Fourth R: research.** Rank five campaign-specific signals by strength and accessibility. Look across the company, its industry and customers, and the contact's professional responsibilities. Start with prior contact evidence, then company pages and public professional statements. For an important unanswered question, try local/trade publications or a relevant searchable database; an empty general search is a coverage gap, not proof of absence. Use [ch05](chapters/ch05-purposeful-search.md) when browsing is available.
4. **Separate fact from inference.** Record what the source says, its URL/source ID, event date if stated, and whether it is a company claim or independent report. Check relevance, timeliness, credibility and bias. Write one possible consequence and one alternative explanation. Hiring may be replacement hiring; an industry trend does not establish this company's pain; a patent does not prove a launch.
5. **Build Sobczak's Possible Value Proposition.** Connect a verified situation to an outcome the offering can actually support. Use tentative language until the prospect confirms the need. Do not calculate savings from invented inputs.
6. **Turn it into a call.** Identity and context → one concrete problem possibility → a short question. Choose context-first/tailored permission from Farrokh and Cegelski only when the context is real. With no verified trigger, use a role-based hypothesis honestly.
7. **Stop and hand off.** Apply Blount's separation of preparation from dialing. Use Richter's CRMT (Client Research Management Tool) principle to keep externally sourced context alongside conversation history, with their origins distinct. Deliver one opening, one priority question, optional follow-ups, and a sensible next step. A lack of public signals means low evidence, not a disqualified account.

## Output and evidence contract

Follow the host schema when present. For Sales Engine's ProspectBrief, use [cheatsheet.md](cheatsheet.md): sourced company/prospect facts, hypotheses, unknowns, relevance, opening, 2–4 questions (one priority required), up to three objections, and nextStep. Preserve supplied source IDs exactly. No sources means company and prospect arrays are empty; CRM context may still inform relevance and a tentative opener.

Outside that schema, produce a compact card: account/role fit; strongest fact with source/date; possible problem; alternative explanation; opener; first question; unknowns; next action. Do not expose irrelevant personal details. Ready-to-say lines must not contain unresolved placeholders: when caller or seller identity is absent, omit that name and state the truthful purpose rather than inventing it.

## Resolve author disagreements

| Tension | Combined rule |
|---|---|
| Deep intelligence vs. call volume | Vary depth by account value and uncertainty. Stop after a defensible reason; batch deeper research outside calling blocks. |
| Research every contact vs. time pressure | No fabricated personalization. An honest role-based hypothesis is acceptable when web evidence is absent. |
| Familiarity vs. honesty | Mention peers or past contact only when approved and true. Verify identity when uncertain. |
| Fact gathering vs. problem discovery | The source establishes an event; the contact establishes its operational impact. |
| Personalization vs. relevance | Keep professional context that changes the business conversation. Do not import the book's family, politics, religion, home-value or inferred-wealth profiling. |
| Durable search methods vs. dated tools | Keep precise queries and source-specific search; verify current capabilities before using old interfaces or operators. Indexed sensitive documents are not prospecting material. |
