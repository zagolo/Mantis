# 4. Hand off a brief and learn from the call

Prepare for the caller's next decision: opening, first question, strongest facts, likely concern and next step. Keep the deeper evidence available without forcing the operator to read it live. Follow the host schema rather than inventing new fields that its UI cannot render.

Richter's Client Research Management Tool (CRMT; ch. XVIII, printed pp. 231–237) combines reusable external intelligence with information learned through conversation. Adapt that principle to the existing brief and source records: identity/current role, relevant business activity, dated external signals, professional responsibilities, possible issue, approved value and unresolved question. It is not a second CRM schema to fill before every call. Keep contact statements and internal history distinct from third-party research; preserve provenance and refresh stale claims before reuse. Exclude the book's personal and sensitive-profile fields from this B2B handoff.

The live coach inherits hypotheses as hypotheses. The prospect can contradict the research. When that happens, update the angle immediately; do not defend the website against the person. A research brief cannot establish qualification, permission to follow up, a booked meeting, or a completed sale.

After a conversation, revise the research question using what the contact actually said. A relevant follow-up might answer that question or supply one useful source, but research does not authorize sending it. Carry the promised action to the application's review workflow. Richter's continuing-research principle extends beyond the first meeting; it is not permission to create unrequested monitoring or outreach.

Review the call using separate outcomes: identity resolved, problem confirmed or disproved, useful next step agreed, meeting held, pilot qualified, and eventual revenue. Meeting count alone can reward unsuitable appointments. Raw dials and researched accounts are activity counts, not conversion evidence.

Measure research time per useful conversation and how often a signal is confirmed. Compare openers within the same ICP/channel/time period with enough conversations to avoid declaring a winner from a few calls. Change one substantive variable at a time.

**Failure mode:** An opener increases meetings but also increases no-shows because it overpromises. Revisit the relevance claim and meeting purpose, not just the sentence wording.

**Connection:** The live coach should use confirmed contact language for discovery and mutual commitments for scheduling.
