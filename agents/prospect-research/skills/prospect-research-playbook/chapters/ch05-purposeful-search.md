# 5. Search for a decision, not a dossier

Use this chapter when a host provides search/page-reading tools or when planning the next research pass. Mantis's brief writer receives evidence from its application; it must not claim to have performed these searches itself.

## Fourth R and query construction

Richter's **Fourth R** is research used to make a conversation relevant. Start with a decision: “Is this the right entity?”, “What operational change is actually described?”, or “Which professional role owns this workflow?” A useful result changes the opener or question. A larger collection of facts is not inherently better.

1. Match the company name to its domain, geography and business. Distinguish headquarters, branch and acquired entity. Confirm the contact's current role before attributing a statement or responsibility.
2. Translate the question into the words a publisher would use. Richter calls this **Webmaster Think**: search for a likely document or page label such as implementation guide, annual report, association directory or service expansion, rather than vague “company pain points.”
3. Start with an exact entity or phrase plus the business issue. Use alternatives for vocabulary, a domain restriction for an established source, or a document-type filter for an intentionally published report. Operator support varies; use the current engine's supported syntax or plain language. Do not assume the 2008 book's search interfaces, cache feature or word-order ranking explanation still apply.
4. Repair a failed query one constraint at a time: try the legal/trading name, relax a phrase, use a synonym, or search the source directly. Too many exact words can hide relevant results; too few can mix unrelated entities. Fewer results alone do not establish higher quality.
5. Open the source and check the proposition, date, entity and publisher. Search snippets and generated summaries are discovery aids; cite only what the evidence actually supports. If the application supplies only a short excerpt, limit the claim to that excerpt and retain missing context as unknown.

## Choose a source for the question

Richter's **Invisible Web** discussion is a reminder that general search coverage is incomplete. It does not justify copying his historical coverage percentages or promising access to every database.

| Question | Useful route | Common false inference |
|---|---|---|
| What changed at this account? | Official newsroom/careers pages; relevant local business publication | Hiring means growth or an urgent problem |
| What matters in its market or its customers' markets? | Trade publication, association, regulator or an accessible industry report | A sector trend is true of every company |
| What is the contact professionally responsible for? | Current company biography and public professional material | Title proves purchasing authority |
| Is an initiative historical or current? | Dated announcements; historical archive if necessary | An old page proves today's plans |
| Does specialized evidence change the case? | Applicable patent/company register or authorized library database | A mention proves ownership; a patent proves a product launch |

For high-value unresolved questions, a library or subscription database can complement public search. Check actual availability and authorization; record access gaps. Do not seek personal financial, political, religious or family data, nor repurpose accidentally exposed internal documents. The useful scope is professional relevance.

## Worked example — original, fictional

Campaign: appointment-routing software for independent outpatient clinics. A current regional trade report says some clinics are centralizing scheduling. Cedar Clinic's own page confirms two sites but says nothing about its routing process.

Keep the two propositions separate. The company fact is “operates two sites”; sector centralization is background context. The hypothesis is “cross-site routing could matter,” with “each site may work independently without friction” as an alternative. Ask: “How do appointment requests get routed between your two sites today?” Do not open with “I saw you are centralizing scheduling” or invent missed appointments. If the contact says the sites are independent and routing works well, retire that angle.

Stop when a further lookup is unlikely to change the decision. Return to [the conversation method](ch03-evidence-to-conversation.md) and [handoff](ch04-handoff-and-feedback.md).
