# 3. Convert evidence into a question

Sobczak's Possible Value Proposition keeps value possible until confirmed. Weinberg makes the client issue the center of the sales story. Farrokh/Cegelski's Problem Proposition makes the issue concrete enough to recognize. Combine them as:

**Observed situation → possible operational consequence → relevant approved capability → question that tests the situation.**

The observation is evidence. The consequence is a hypothesis. The capability must come from campaign facts. The question must permit a negative answer. Do not hide a product pitch inside a question or ask the buyer to validate a made-up diagnosis.

Example: two coordinator vacancies may imply request volume, staff turnover, or a coverage change. Ask why those roles are being added; then ask where work gets stuck if the answer reveals a problem. Avoid demanding cost numbers before the person has agreed to discuss the workflow.

Choose the strongest single angle. Multiple unrelated facts make the caller sound like they are reciting a dossier. Identity, one relevant observation and a question usually carry more value than a biography or vague praise.

Richter separates company-information and industry-information telephone approaches (ch. XVIII, printed pp. 238–240). Preserve that distinction:

- **Company evidence:** attribute the specific event or executive statement, connect it to an approved capability, then ask whether the possible issue matters. Do not put another executive's words in the contact's mouth.
- **Industry evidence:** name the sector context and ask whether it applies here. Never say “you are struggling with…” because a trade survey says some peers struggle.

His scripts are adaptable guides, not instructions to demand a 90-second monologue or a 45-minute meeting. Use the host's shorter opening and the campaign's actual next step. Evidence-backed warmth means relevance; it does not mean there is an existing relationship, consent, or an agreed need. Customer names and quantified success stories in any source's sample script are not approved proof for our offering.

For repeat contact, honor the recorded conversation and requested timing. “Our team last spoke about…” can be accurate when another caller handled it; “You told me…” would not be. A missed dial supports neither sentence.
