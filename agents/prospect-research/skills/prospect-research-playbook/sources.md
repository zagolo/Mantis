# Sources and coverage

Compiled and updated 2026-09-18. Selection is based on usefulness for this workflow, not an objective ranking of all sales books. All five local originals are now available. Four books use prior book-to-skill analysis plus targeted original checks; Richter's newly supplied book uses full-file extraction and targeted section synthesis. This is a combined operational skill, not an exhaustive conversion of every chapter or illustration. See the repository's `docs/sales-playbooks/book-source-notes.md` and `docs/sales-playbooks/richter-book-foldin.md` for extraction and coverage details.

Simplified.*; their PDF image coverage was not measured. Richter's pypdf pass yielded 79,829 source-body words (79,857 including the converter wrapper); warnings identified ten undecodable image objects, and screenshots were not comprehensively inspected. ## Verified public companions

- [Sam Richter: Qualifying Prospects with Better Sales Intelligence](https://oneofakindsales.com/sam-richter-better-sales-intelligence/) — Conversational Selling, host-published interview transcript actually retrieved. Contributes purposeful search, date filtering, relevance and the “three by five” heuristic. This is a podcast transcript, not a full-book substitute.
- [Richter's official book page](https://www.samrichter.com/book) — confirms title and provides a publisher/author-hosted sample and ebook route. Marketing and sample availability do not establish that all book content was read.
- [Blount: How Much Research Preparation Should You Do Before a Cold Call?](https://salesgravy.com/how-much-research-should-you-do-before-a-cold-call-ask-jeb/) — full article retrieved; [associated video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lK8enndO1YQ), 26 Nov 2025, actual transcript retrieved. Supports batching research and varying preparation depth. Its two-to-three-minute guidance is practitioner advice, not a universal optimum.
- [Sobczak: “Socially Engineering” Your Prospecting Calls](https://salesgravy.com/socially-engineering-your-prospecting-calls/) — full author article retrieved; contributes truthful identity, business reason and a specific routing question. No impersonation or false relationship claim is adopted.
- [Gong/Farrokh: Insights from 300M Calls](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls) — full article retrieved; contributes timing/fit prioritization and separate connect/meeting measures. Observational vendor data, not causal proof.
- [30MPC masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2vivv2HeiBU) — 20 Jan 2026; actual transcript retrieved. Connects relevant research to the opener and Problem Proposition. Prospect audio in the examples was recreated with AI voices according to the publisher; these are not unaltered recordings.
- [Keenan: Find the Gap, Measure the Gap](https://www.asalesguy.com/find-the-gap-measure-the-gap/) — author article actually retrieved. Supplemental cross-stage check: current-to-desired-state differences require buyer discovery; research alone cannot price the gap.

Additional verified articles and video transcripts are cataloged in the repository's `docs/sales-playbooks/auxiliary-source-notes.md`. Their applied decision rules are distilled into this skill; full third-party transcripts remain in ignored local retrieval files.

## Attribution and maintenance

PVP belongs to Sobczak; Problem Proposition, Tailored Permission and Mr. Miyagi belong to Farrokh/Cegelski; client-issue targeting and Power Statement belong to Weinberg; prospecting discipline and Anchor–Disrupt–Ask belong to Blount. Fourth R, Webmaster Think and CRMT belong to Richter; his “Invisible Web” chapter credits Chris Sherman and Gary Price for that term. Evidence/hypothesis/alternative separation, the runtime field mapping, time-budget defaults and adaptations below are this engine's synthesis.

Richter's 2008 tool directions were not tested against current websites. Retain deliberate queries, source-specific discovery and research organization; do not import old interface names, search-cache features, price/access claims, search-engine coverage percentages, or benchmark promises. His 10–15 minute traditional-call ceiling (printed p. 253) is historical practitioner guidance; this engine's shorter defaults remain tunable. The later interview's “three by five” heuristic is not attributed to this edition.

Scope choices: use professional context, excluding personal wealth, family, political and religious profiling and accidentally exposed sensitive documents. Mention an advertising/referral relationship only when true. Use sector trends and job/patent clues as hypotheses rather than proof of a specific need. Scripts are shortened to host limits, and book examples' customer results are never treated as proof for the user's offering. These are explicit modern B2B adaptations, not claims that the original book prescribed them.
