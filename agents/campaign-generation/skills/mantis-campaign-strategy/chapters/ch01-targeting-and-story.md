# Target and story

Weinberg's New Sales Driver starts with targets before sales weapons or execution. Apply that order to the offering brief: who is this for, what issue could matter, and what can the seller actually do? A narrow coherent campaign creates reusable language and makes later results interpretable.

His Power Statement contains a headline and transition, then client issues, offerings and differentiators. Use the sequence as a thinking tool. Do not force its full length or component counts into Mantis's short card. If the operator has not established a differentiator, omit the superiority claim rather than inventing one.

Sobczak's PVP keeps the bridge to value provisional. Farrokh/Cegelski's Problem Proposition makes the work situation recognizable. These methods serve different purposes: one establishes a possible reason to care; the other expresses it plainly. Use one combined line rather than consecutive introductions from multiple authors.

For example, “Maintenance requests can lose context between a resident-services inbox and a coordinator” is a possible situation. “Your residents are angry because your team loses requests” is an unsupported account diagnosis. “We map that handoff before proposing a pilot” is usable only if the operator supplied it as a real capability.

Blount's preparation discipline supports writing this strategy before the dialing block. It does not justify a model imposing a numeric call quota, opening hours or a universal buyer persona.

Connect this campaign-level language to verified individual evidence in the prospect-research stage. A campaign generator cannot manufacture that evidence itself.
