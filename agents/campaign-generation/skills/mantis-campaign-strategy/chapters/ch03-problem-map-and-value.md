# Problem map and value of the next step

## Prepare a PIC without inventing a prospect

Keenan's **Problem Identification Chart (PIC)** maps **problem → impact → root cause** before outreach. Start from the operator's real offering and possible issues it can address. Identify the affected workflow and role, then choose one credible hypothesis to test. A generic campaign cannot assert a particular company has that issue, nor can a sector stereotype establish impact or readiness.

Distinguish a **technical problem** (a broken process, tool or handoff) from a **business problem** (the consequence for the organization). The distinction helps ask a useful follow-up; it does not prove causality. Avoid promising that an integration will resolve a staffing, policy or prioritization problem.

### Original worked map

Suppose the approved offer is mapping maintenance-request handoffs before deciding whether a pilot makes sense. The target is residential property-management operations. No software integration, price or ROI has been supplied.

| PIC component | Campaign hypothesis | What would validate or reject it |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Request ownership may become unclear between inbox and coordinator | Ask how the next owner is assigned; accept that the process may work well |
| Impact | Unclear ownership may delay an urgent request | Ask what happens if ownership is delayed, without asserting missed service or revenue |
| Root cause | Ownership rules or handoff context may be unclear | Ask how exceptions are routed; do not assume a software integration is the cure |

The resulting opener can name the handoff and offer a short explanation. It cannot say their tenants are unhappy or promise a salary saving. A useful next step can map one real request to check whether a pilot has a purpose. If the workflow is outside the offer, do not invent a new capability to maintain the hypothesis.

## Select the next information need

Keenan's discovery questions serve different purposes:

- **Probing:** make a relevant answer specific, such as what “slow” means.
- **Process:** understand how people, data or ownership move through the workflow.
- **Provoking:** gently test a possible consequence or alternative without assuming damage.
- **Validating:** check that the interpretation matches the buyer's meaning.

Mantis generates only 3–4 campaign questions. Choose questions that fit the offering and objective, with optional follow-ups; do not mechanically include every type or force a full current-state interview into the opening. The live coach should adapt to answers. Current state, desired future state and the reason for change become prospect-specific through actual evidence.

Keenan prioritizes impact over counting problems when selecting prospects. At campaign creation, that is a target-selection hypothesis. Do not assign verified pain scores or A–D prospect classifications without evidence; apparent scale can hide a small actual gap.

## Make the next action worth taking

**Offer − Ask = Value** means compare the actual benefit to the buyer with the time, effort and risk requested. It is a qualitative judgment, not numerical ROI. A broad product presentation may offer little value; a limited fit-check may answer a real question. Reduce the ask or improve the genuine benefit when the exchange is weak. Do not invent a free report, reference, benchmark or implementation promise.

When an engaged buyer hesitates about a next action, Blount's *Objections* **Ledge → Explain value → Ask** is appropriate: acknowledge the concern, explain the action's real value, then ask if useful. That is different from prospecting **Ledge → Disrupt → Ask** and the buying-commitment **Relate → Isolate and clarify → Minimize → Ask → Fall back to an alternative**. Generic campaign objection lines address interruptions; they should not pretend discovery or a purchase decision has occurred.

For example, if a buyer questions why a workflow owner should attend, the supported benefit may be first-hand context about handoffs. It is not “our specialist will find 30% savings” unless independently approved and justified. If a person refuses, end; a perceived high-value offer does not override willingness.

## What stays outside this role

No prospective revenue, costs, emotional state, technical compatibility or buying authority becomes a known fact through campaign planning. No universal budget gate, mandatory executive title or numerical gap threshold belongs here. A genuine offering constraint can become an affirmative criterion; a specific contact's answer will later determine its value.
