# Reusable coaching patterns

## Evidence to question

**When:** A relevant public signal exists but its business impact is unconfirmed.

**How:** Use one dated, correctly matched fact; connect it to a possible problem; ask whether that problem exists. When an inference carries the message, use uncertainty naturally. Store the answer separately from the research.

**Trade-off:** Enough specificity earns attention; overclaiming breaks trust. Sobczak's PVP preserves uncertainty while Farrokh/Cegelski's Problem Proposition makes the situation concrete.

## One conversational decision

**When:** The caller wants to explain, discover and book in one breath.

**How:** Ask first for the next appropriate decision: permission to explain, recognition of a problem, or interest in a next step. Offer times only after interest. Keep one question per cue.

**Trade-off:** More turns can be appropriate because each answer changes the recommendation. Do not force a rigid sequence when the buyer voluntarily asks for the next step.

## Acknowledge, clarify once, respond

**When:** A first ambiguous brush-off occurs and the person remains willing to talk.

**How:** Acknowledge the specific concern, ask one relevant clarification, then use the answer. If they decline or repeat the brush-off, end. Firm refusal and DNC skip clarification.

**Trade-off:** This adopts the conversational purpose of Mr. Miyagi and Anchor/Ledge–Disrupt–Ask while imposing an explicit engine boundary on persistence. It is not the books' unrestricted repeat-objection rule.

## Workflow before impact

**When:** The prospect agrees to discuss a potential problem.

**How:** Understand what actually happens today; ask what becomes difficult; then identify one consequence and its cause. Ask for the desired state only after enough current context. Validate summaries in the buyer's words.

**Trade-off:** Keenan's verified PIC and gap model guide diagnosis, not an interrogation sequence. Stop gathering when the next action is clear or willingness ends.

## Select a question by the information gap

**When:** A receptive buyer's answer leaves something important unclear.

**How:** Choose probing for a specific fact, process for the handoff, provoking for a possible consequence/alternative, or validating for interpretation. Ask only the next useful question. Technical symptoms lead to business consequences only when evidence supports the link.

**Trade-off:** A standard checklist can omit the buyer's most important issue. Keenan's types guide the information needed; they are not four compulsory questions or a license to infer emotional state.

## Choose the objection stage

**When:** An objection appears during interruption, next-step discussion or an actual purchase decision.

**How:** Select Ledge–Disrupt–Ask for prospecting, Ledge–Explain value–Ask for micro-commitments, or Relate–Isolate and clarify–Minimize–Ask–Fall back to an alternative for buying commitment. Use only the next justified move. Explain genuine buyer value for another meeting; diagnose an actual commercial concern before prescribing an answer.

**Trade-off:** Applying purchase-closing pressure to a stranger's interruption refusal is a category error. Fallback does not authorize another ask after firm refusal. Preserve the exact five-step formulation: isolate and clarify are one stage and fallback is fifth.

## Check value without inventing ROI

**When:** Assessing a desired result or proposing a next meeting.

**How:** For a quantitative gap, align current/desired units and periods; keep desired outcome separate from recoverable benefit. For a meeting, use Offer − Ask = Value qualitatively: explain the relevant benefit and scale the time/effort appropriately.

**Trade-off:** A target gap is not a causal promise and a modest ask can still lack value. A small gap, real affordability constraint or unaddressable root cause may support ending the pursuit.

## Truthful uncertainty

**When:** The operator is asked for a price, integration or result not supported by the campaign.

**How:** State what is known and what needs checking; agree a practical way to verify it if the prospect wants to continue. Do not fill the silence with a plausible number or roadmap promise.

**Trade-off:** A narrower accurate answer is more useful than a confident unsupported pitch. A capability gap may disqualify the opportunity.

## Mutual next step

**When:** Both sides see a useful reason to continue.

**How:** Name the specific outcome of the next conversation; identify who should attend; agree time and time zone; summarize each person's contribution. Propose the correct calendar intent without implying execution.

**Trade-off:** Sobczak's action commitment improves clarity. Booking a meeting with no relevant purpose inflates counts but may reduce attendance and trust.

## Silence as coaching

**When:** The contact is explaining, the caller has already asked a good question, or no reliable transcript is available.

**How:** Withhold a cue. After a clear completed turn, respond to its meaning rather than the previous hypothesis. Use sequence state to avoid stale suggestions.

**Trade-off:** The agent may visibly do less while making the operator more attentive. Cue count is not a quality metric.

## One behavior improvement loop

**When:** Reviewing a set of calls from one campaign/persona.

**How:** Separate connection, relevance, discovery, next-step and attendance failures. Select one observable behavior; rehearse it with difficult variants; inspect subsequent calls and outcomes. Preserve positive examples and counterexamples.

**Trade-off:** Small samples guide practice but do not establish causal conversion lifts. List quality, time zones and offer changes can confound comparisons.

## Learn a decision from a call example

**When:** Studying the retrieved 30MPC masterclass transcript (T01) or Keenan talk transcript (T04).

**How:** Identify the context that made the next question useful; reconstruct the choice with a different honest account fact or buyer answer; rehearse how the move changes when the fact is corrected. T01 supports context before permission and a meeting purpose derived from the response. T04 supports understanding the current state before claiming future value.

**Trade-off:** Teaching demonstrations are not independent conversion evidence. T01's prospect voices were recreated with AI; captions do not prove acoustic delivery or preserve every nuance. Learn the decision rule instead of copying a trainer's customer results or aggressive phrasing.
