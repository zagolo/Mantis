# Objections and boundaries

## Core idea

Objection wording does not reveal hidden intent. First identify whether the person wants to continue and what decision they are considering. A first-call interruption, a request for another meeting and a contract concern require different responses.

## First decision: should the conversation continue?

- **Explicit stop-contact request:** acknowledge and close immediately; recommend `do_not_contact`. No qualification question or callback ask.
- **Firm refusal or repeated brush-off:** end politely. “No, I do not want to discuss this” is sufficient. It need not contain the words “do not contact,” and does not by itself establish a wider DNC request.
- **Ambiguous first brush-off, still engaged:** at most one respectful clarification. Check prior turns so the same question is not repeated under different wording.
- **Substantive concern, willing to discuss:** answer with approved information or choose one question that resolves the missing context. Their answer changes the next cue.

## Match Blount's framework to the decision

| Context | Exact framework from *Objections* | How to apply across turns |
|---|---|---|
| **Prospecting objection**: resistance to an interruption | **Ledge → Disrupt → Ask** | Pause with a specific acknowledgment; remove the expected argument; ask one relevant, welcome clarification or modest next step. A ledge steadies the seller; it is not automatic agreement with a false claim. |
| **Micro-commitment objection**: hesitation about a next action in an engaged process | **Ledge → Explain value → Ask** | Acknowledge the cost/concern, explain the buyer's concrete benefit from that step, then ask whether it is worthwhile. Explain emotional, insight or tangible value only when real and supported. |
| **Buying commitment objection**: unresolved concern at a real purchase decision | **Relate → Isolate and clarify → Minimize → Ask → Fall back to an alternative** | Relate to the concern; identify other concerns and priority; clarify meaning; address the risk using evidence and agreed outcomes; ask for a justified decision; offer a smaller mutually useful alternative only if welcome. Isolate and clarify form one stage; fallback is the fifth. Do not perform all five moves in one cue. |
| **Red herring**: an apparent side issue | **PAIS — Pause, Acknowledge, Ignore, Save** | Preserve the name for reference. In Mantis, pause and acknowledge; answer direct material questions or agree to revisit later. Do not silently ignore pricing, capability, trust or contact-source concerns. |

Blount's earlier *Fanatical Prospecting* uses **Anchor → Disrupt → Ask** for RBOs. Farrokh/Cegelski's **Mr. Miyagi Method** is **Agree → Incentivize conversation → Sell the test drive**. These share composure and reduced confrontation; they are not consecutive scripts to stack.

For buying decisions, avoid Blount's **pump and pounce** failure: answering the first objection before knowing its meaning or whether another concern matters more. “Minimize” is the author's name for reducing the objection's perceived weight through the buyer's reasons and relevant proof. Here it means honestly addressing uncertainty and risk; never dismiss the concern, exaggerate the cost of inaction, manufacture a deadline, or treat an earlier yes as irrevocable consent.

Keenan's later-stage comparison of objections against desired outcomes is useful only when those outcomes were actually confirmed. Ask how the new requirement affects the desired result; accept that it may reveal a genuine constraint or correct the original diagnosis. Do not force buyers to defend a refusal or assume the objection is false.

## Practical decisions

| Situation | Useful direction | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Busy | Respect the constraint; offer a callback only if welcome | They must leave or decline |
| Existing vendor | Acknowledge what works; ask about one relevant limitation if receptive | Satisfied and unwilling to explore |
| No budget | If welcome, clarify whether timing, allocation, affordability or priority is the issue | Do not assume money exists because value might |
| Send information | Honor the requested topic; clarify only if necessary and welcome | No automatic meeting or fabricated interest |
| Price | Give approved price/range plainly and explain limits | Do not evade a known price with discovery |
| Missing capability | State actual capability; check whether essential | An essential unsupported feature can be a real mismatch |
| Source of number | Give supplied provenance or admit uncertainty | Stop immediately if requested |

## Original worked examples

**Prospecting:** “We have software for that.” If still engaged and handoffs are relevant, one cue could be: “Makes sense. Does it cover request handoffs too, or mainly the records?” If the answer is “Everything, and we're not interested,” end. If they describe a manual handoff, explore that stated issue. The software brand alone proves nothing.

**Micro-commitment:** After agreeing a routing issue exists, the buyer asks why the coordinator should attend. If the approved meeting really maps the workflow, explain that the coordinator can show where ownership changes, then ask if that would be useful. Do not promise an audit report, free implementation or guaranteed savings to secure attendance.

**Buying commitment:** At an actual pilot discussion the buyer says, “The rollout worries me.” First relate: “That is a fair concern. What part of the rollout feels riskiest?” If they say interruption to the weekend team, clarify the requirement and whether other issues matter. An approved staged rollout can address that risk; if none exists, state the gap and verify feasibility. Do not pretend the pilot is risk-free or skip directly to a signature.

**Refusal:** “Please stop calling.” A suitable cue is “Understood. I'll respect that. Goodbye.” Nothing else is needed.

## Runtime and source boundaries

Use `clarify`, `listen` or `qualify` for the first ambiguous response; `warning` and stage `closed` for firm/repeated refusal and DNC; DNC additionally recommends `do_not_contact`. Refusal alone does not support `disqualified` or a negative fit criterion. Objection labels must be configured or null.

The one-clarification limit is Mantis's stricter synthesis. *Objections* permits additional turnaround attempts in places and describes calling back after a hang-up; those tactics are not adopted. Its empirical/neuroscience explanations and illustrative conversion fractions are not independently validated benchmarks.
