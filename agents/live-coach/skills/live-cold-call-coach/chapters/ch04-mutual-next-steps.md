# Mutual next steps

## Core idea

The next step should be valuable enough that both sides choose it. On a complex B2B service, a cold call commonly earns discovery or pilot scoping; it does not establish a closed deal by itself.

## Pick the appropriate objective

Sobczak's primary/secondary objectives help distinguish the intended buyer action from useful alternative progress. A secondary objective might be a requested callback or better routing, never an excuse to continue after refusal. Farrokh/Cegelski's **Interest-Based CTA** separates willingness to explore from calendar details. Their **test drive** means the next conversation has value before any purchase commitment.

Blount's **Ledge → Explain value → Ask** applies when an engaged buyer hesitates about the next action. Explain the benefit to them, not the seller's need to finish a stage. The book distinguishes emotional value (less uncertainty), insight value (better understanding) and tangible value (an actual useful artifact/experience). Promise only what the campaign supports.

Keenan's **Offer − Ask = Value** is a qualitative exchange: does the benefit justify the time, effort and risk requested? If not, improve the real benefit or reduce the ask. It is not a formula for invented ROI. A request to “hear about our product” may have little buyer value; a specific fit-check can be useful when it resolves their stated question.

Use these decisions:

1. **No relevant problem or interest:** close politely or use campaign-defined disqualification. A meeting quota is not a reason to invent value.
2. **Possible relevance but no time:** ask whether a callback or short discovery conversation is useful, if welcome. A requested callback is not a booked shared meeting.
3. **Confirmed issue and willingness to explore:** propose a meeting with a specific purpose, such as mapping one workflow and testing technical fit.
4. **Buyer wants a pilot:** establish what would be tested, who owns the process, success criteria, constraints, commercial scope and decision authority. If those are incomplete, propose a scoping conversation; do not invent a price or promise implementation.
5. **Buyer wants to proceed immediately:** follow the campaign's authorized process and approved terms. A cue cannot execute a contract or turn an unapproved promise into a real offer.

## Original illustrative example

The contact confirms duplicate data entry is causing missed requests and asks whether the proposed service could handle their workflow. A useful next step could be: “Would it be useful to map one request end to end with your operations lead and check where a small pilot could fit?”

Only after agreement should the caller ask about time. The subsequent invitation should describe the agreed mapping and fit check. It should not become a generic demo or claim that the pilot has already been purchased.

If the contact says “Email me a summary first,” clarify any missing topic only if welcome, then reflect that actual next step. Do not send a placeholder meeting as though they had agreed to attend.

## Calendar and action discipline

Confirm the exact date, time and time zone. Relative expressions such as “Thursday afternoon” are incomplete until the intended window is clear. Use supplied availability; do not assume the operator is free. Confirm the actual attendee email when needed instead of fabricating it from the person's name.

- `meeting`: a shared slot mutually agreed for a defined purpose; attendees only when their details are supplied.
- `callback`: the contact requested a return call; an operator task, not a prospect meeting invitation.
- `reminder`: an operator-only nudge, such as preparation before an agreed meeting.

The model drafts; the engine's approval flow governs execution. Do not say the invitation was sent based on a draft field. If the schema cannot represent an uncertainty faithfully, omit the proposal and cue the operator to clarify it.

## What to hand over

Capture the contact's words about the issue, consequence, desired outcome, constraints and agreed action. Record who does what and by when. Separate confirmed statements, seller commitments, hypotheses and unresolved questions. A polite “sounds interesting” is interest, not confirmed authority, budget or an accepted event.

Keenan's supplied book and public gap article link value with cost, effort and risk. Apply this when choosing the smallest useful pilot scope: the experiment should test a real uncertainty with an agreed measure, not promise that the largest imagined saving will occur.

## Failure modes

- Asking for calendar slots before the contact understands the purpose.
- Treating an information request as a meeting acceptance.
- Turning a callback into an attendee invitation.
- Offering arbitrary free work, discounts or guarantees to rescue a weak fit.
- Counting a tentative next step as a closed sale.
